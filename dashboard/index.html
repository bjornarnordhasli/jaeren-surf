How the forecast is made

The same model runs for every spot

  • Waves: BarentsWatch Bølgevarsel (Jæren model): wave height, peak period and direction at a grid point about 5 km out to sea from each spot.
  • 6-day option: BarentsWatch runs about 2½ days ahead. After that the waves come from Météo-France's MFWAM model (via Open-Meteo) at the nearest model cell, scaled to match BarentsWatch where the two overlap (height, peak period from mean period, direction). It is coarser and further ahead, so treat days 3–6 as a trend rather than a session plan.
  • Wind: MET Norway's locationforecast, the forecast behind yr, at each spot.
  • Swell vs wind sea: BarentsWatch gives the total sea only, so the local wind sea is estimated from the yr wind (fetch-limited wave growth, open-sea fetch for onshore winds) and the rest is treated as swell.
  • Bathymetry: EMODnet Bathymetry DTM (about 60 × 115 m cells). Kartverket's finer depth models don't cover Jæren's inshore waters, so reef and sandbar detail is smoothed out.
  • Breaking: linear wave rays refract and shoal over the seabed; a wave breaks where its height exceeds 0.78 × depth. The rating weighs breaker size (full marks for 1.5–2 m breakers, tapering off for smaller and bigger waves), period, wind direction against the spot's own shoreline, swell share, breaker type and how much the wave peels along the depth contours.
  • Spot positions are Surfline's; each spot's facing direction comes from the seabed slope around it. Tide is not included (range under half a metre here).
